abstract = "Previous research has documented unethical behavior in and by groups (see Mannix, Neal, Tenbrunsel, 2006, for a review). In situations where individuals behave cooperatively, groups often defect from such behavior to protect or enhance the group (Wildschut, Pinter, Vevea, Insko, Schopler, 2003; Morgan, Tindale, 2002). Thus, groups are likely to use the group's welfare to guide their moral compass and behave in ways consistent with their self-interest even when it violates typical norms of ethics (Cohen, Gunia, Kim-Jun, Murnighan, 2009). We argue that this group tendency stems from the group enhancement/protection norm (Tindale, 2008), which leads groups to behave in ways that enhance/protect the group even when members may find such behavior ethically suspect. We also attempted to change this tendency by changing the definition or mission of the group. Participants acted as individuals or in four-person groups and were asked to play the role of an executive board member of a pharmaceutical company. They were told their company had a legally approved drug on the market that was very profitable but had potentially deadly side effects. Participants were told that the FDA was considering banning the drug and the decision involved how the company should respond. The scenario gave the decision maker options that ranged from pulling the drug off the market immediately (most ethical) to continuing to strongly market the drug and lobbying the FDA to prevent status changes (least ethical). Participants were given as honesty/trustworthy company mission, a competitive/profit oriented company mission, or no mission. After reading the scenario and mission, all participants worked individually and privately stated their preferred option. Individuals were then asked to list the reasons for their decisions and asked to re-evaluate their initial preference. Group members were asked to discuss the case and reach a group consensus as to the best action. Final decisions were subjected to a 2 (group vs. individual) x 3 (mission statement: no statement, competitive, client-orientated) ANOVA. Results showed a main effect for the group/individual condition. That is, groups choose a more unethical decision on average when compared to individuals. Confidence ratings were also analyzed with a 2 (group vs. individual) x 3 (mission statement: no statement, competitive, client-orientated) ANOVA. Results demonstrated a main effect for the group/individual condition. Specifically, when compared to individuals, groups were more confident in their decision on average. Changing the mission of the group did not moderate either effect."
abstract_short = "Groups and individuals played the role of an executive board member of a pharmaceutical company whose product was found to have deadly side effects. Participants chose among various ethical decisions options. Groups were more likely to choose unethical options and were more confident in their choices compared to individuals."
